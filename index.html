    How the score works

    • Glare counts when the sun is between the horizon and 25° up, and within 30° left or right of the direction of travel.
    • The score is highest when the sun is dead ahead and under 15° up, where a visor cannot block it.
    • Both directions of a two-way road are checked. One-way roads are checked in their direction only.
    • Risk is the glare score raised near places where a driver must see clearly: 60% higher at traffic signals, 50% at junctions between main roads, 40% at crossings and schools. Small rings mark them when you zoom in.
    • A typed trip uses a public route service (OSRM). A trip picked by tapping follows the quickest path on the loaded roads. Either way the sun moves as the trip goes on.
    • Cloud cover comes from the Open-Meteo hourly forecast, for the past week and the next 16 days. At 85% cloud or more no glare is shown, and between 60% and 85% it is halved. Outside those dates a clear sky is assumed.
    • Not included yet: hills, buildings and trees that block the sun, and wet-road reflection.
    • Times are US Central. The sun position is calculated for the middle of the loaded roads.
